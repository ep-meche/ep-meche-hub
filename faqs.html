Degree Planning & Focus Areas
Do I need to declare a Mechanical Engineering focus area?

Yes. Students should complete the Mechanical Engineering Focus Area Track Declaration when beginning to plan their coursework.

Review the Focus Areas page to explore the available focus areas, course maps, and related coursework.

Can I change my focus area later?

Yes. If you are considering changing your focus area, work directly with your academic advisor to review your degree progress and remaining requirements.

Your advisor can help determine how a change in focus area may affect your planned coursework and ensure that any necessary adjustments support your academic goals and anticipated graduation timeline.

How do I know which courses count toward my focus area?

Start by reviewing the Focus Areas page and the course map provided for your selected focus area. These resources can help you identify courses that align with your focus area and plan your path through the program.

Work with your academic advisor to develop a course plan that supports your academic and professional goals while keeping you on track for graduation. Be sure to verify the official Degree Requirements applicable to your catalog year when making course selections.

How many courses are required for the master's degree?

Review the current Degree Requirements page for the official requirements applicable to your catalog year.

Is 535.641 Mathematical Methods for Engineers required in the first semester?

535.641 Mathematical Methods for Engineers is commonly used in early degree planning, but students should follow their individual degree plan and advising guidance that applies to their situation.

Review the Degree Requirements page and your degree audit to confirm what is required for your program.

Courses & Registration
How do I register for courses?

Check the Academic Calendar for registration dates, then register through SIS.

Where can I see which courses are offered in an upcoming semester?

Use the Course Offering Schedule to plan your coursework across future semesters.

When preparing to register, use the EP Courses page to view current course offerings, schedules, and available sections. Course offerings are subject to change, so be sure to review the EP Courses page when planning your registration for an upcoming semester.

Is the Course Offering Schedule guaranteed?

No. The Course Offering Schedule is intended as a planning resource and may change based on program scheduling needs.

Be sure to review the EP Courses page for current offerings when preparing to register.

What should I do if a course I want is full?

If a course is full, register for the waitlist. If a seat becomes available, you will receive a notification with instructions to accept the available spot.

Joining the waitlist is also important for course planning. Waitlist demand helps the program assess student interest and determine whether an additional section may be needed and can be offered that semester. For this reason, students who are interested in a full course are encouraged to join the waitlist rather than simply checking back for an opening.

What if SIS will not let me register?

For enrollment or account-management issues, use Student Case Management (SEAM).

Can I take courses outside of Mechanical Engineering?

Yes. The Mechanical Engineering Degree Requirements allow students to take courses outside of the Mechanical Engineering program as part of their elective coursework.

At least two of the four electives must be from a core engineering discipline listed below, and at most two electives may be chosen from other programs:

  • Applied and Computational Mathematics
  • Applied Biomedical Engineering
  • Applied Physics
  • Artificial Intelligence
  • Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering
  • Civil Engineering
  • Computer Science
  • Data Science
  • Electrical and Computer Engineering
  • Materials Science and Engineering
  • Mechanical Engineering
  • Robotics and Autonomous Systems

Before registering for a course outside of Mechanical Engineering, consult with your academic advisor to confirm that the course will apply appropriately toward your degree requirements and overall course plan.

Thesis & Independent Study Planning
Can I complete a thesis as part of my master's degree?

Yes. Students may pursue the thesis option, which consists of a coordinated sequence of eight courses plus a 6-credit master's thesis.

Key requirements include:

  • Prior approval is required. Students must obtain written approval from both their academic advisor and the Program Chair before pursuing the thesis option.
  • An approved research advisor is required. The research advisor may be a current full-time faculty member in the Johns Hopkins Department of Mechanical Engineering or a current instructor in the Engineering for Professionals Mechanical Engineering program.
  • Thesis research typically begins after focus-area coursework is complete and should generally be completed within no more than three consecutive semesters.
  • Students complete a two-course thesis sequence: EN.535.820 Master’s Graduate Research and EN.535.821 Master’s Graduate Thesis, for a total of 6 thesis credits.

Students interested in the thesis option should begin by discussing their plans with their academic advisor to ensure the thesis pathway can be appropriately incorporated into their degree plan.

How do I get started with a thesis?

Begin by reviewing the Thesis Process Guidelines, which outline the steps for developing and submitting your thesis proposal.

Once your proposal is complete, complete the Thesis Approval Form and obtain all required signatures.

Submit the completed proposal and approval form through SEAM Student Case Management for review and enrollment in the appropriate thesis course.

What is an Independent Study, and how do I enroll?

An Independent Study is an individually tailored, supervised project on a subject related to mechanical engineering. The project content and expectations are developed and agreed upon by the student and faculty sponsor.

Key requirements include:

  • Timing: Independent Study may only be taken during the second half of a student's master's degree program.
  • Faculty supervision: All Independent Studies must be supervised by a current Mechanical Engineering instructor. Exceptions require approval from the Mechanical Engineering Program Chair.
  • Prior coursework: The Independent Study must build upon material from prior Mechanical Engineering coursework.
  • Proposal and approval: Students must complete the Independent Study Project Proposal Form and obtain all required signatures.

Once the proposal and approval form are complete, submit the materials through SEAM Student Case Management for review and enrollment.
