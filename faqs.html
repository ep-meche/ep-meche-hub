Degree Planning & Focus Areas
Do I need to declare a Mechanical Engineering focus area?

Yes. Students should complete the Mechanical Engineering Focus Area Track Declaration when beginning to plan their coursework.

Review the Focus Areas page to explore the available focus areas, course maps, and related coursework.

Can I change my focus area later?

Yes. If you are considering changing your focus area, work directly with your academic advisor to review your degree progress and remaining requirements.

Your advisor can help determine how a change in focus area may affect your planned coursework and ensure that any necessary adjustments support your academic goals and anticipated graduation timeline.

How do I know which courses count toward my focus area?

Start by reviewing the Focus Areas page and the course map provided for your selected focus area. These resources can help you identify courses that align with your focus area and plan your path through the program.

Work with your academic advisor to develop a course plan that supports your academic and professional goals while keeping you on track for graduation. Be sure to verify the official Degree Requirements applicable to your catalog year when making course selections.

How many courses are required for the master's degree?

Review the current Degree Requirements page for the official requirements applicable to your catalog year.

Is 535.641 Mathematical Methods for Engineers required in the first semester?

535.641 Mathematical Methods for Engineers is commonly used in early degree planning, but students should follow their individual degree plan and advising guidance that applies to their situation.

Review the Degree Requirements page and your degree audit to confirm what is required for your program.

Courses & Registration
How do I register for courses?

Check the Academic Calendar for registration dates, then register through SIS.

Where can I see which courses are offered in an upcoming semester?

Use the Course Offering Schedule to plan your coursework across future semesters.

When preparing to register, use the EP Courses page to view current course offerings, schedules, and available sections. Course offerings are subject to change, so be sure to review the EP Courses page when planning your registration for an upcoming semester.

Is the Course Offering Schedule guaranteed?

No. The Course Offering Schedule is intended as a planning resource and may change based on program scheduling needs.

Be sure to review the EP Courses page for current offerings when preparing to register.

What should I do if a course I want is full?

If a course is full, register for the waitlist. If a seat becomes available, you will receive a notification with instructions to accept the available spot.

Joining the waitlist is also important for course planning. Waitlist demand helps the program assess student interest and determine whether an additional section may be needed and can be offered that semester. For this reason, students who are interested in a full course are encouraged to join the waitlist rather than simply checking back for an opening.

Can I take courses outside of Mechanical Engineering?

Review the published Degree Requirements before planning courses outside the program.

If you are unsure whether a course can be applied to your degree, consult with your academic advisor before registering.

Thesis, Independent Study & Special Planning
Can I complete a thesis as part of my master's degree?

Review the current Mechanical Engineering degree requirements and available thesis guidance before making this decision.

Work with your academic advisor to determine how a thesis would fit within your overall degree plan and anticipated graduation timeline.

How do I get started with a thesis?

Begin by reviewing the program's thesis guidance and discussing your plans with your academic advisor.

Your advisor can help you understand how the thesis fits into your degree requirements and identify the appropriate next steps.

What is an Independent Study?

An Independent Study is a faculty-supervised academic experience built around an approved topic or area of study.

Students should work with the appropriate faculty member and confirm the registration and approval process before enrolling.
