Mechanical Engineering Focus Areas

A focus area must be selected as part of the Mechanical Engineering master's program. Your focus area helps narrow your course planning, but it does not appear on your degree.

Use the sections below to explore each focus area and the types of topics covered. See the Mechanical Engineering course listings for current course options and formats.

Explore the Focus Areas

Advanced Manufacturing

Study the automation of design and manufacturing systems, including computer-aided design, engineering, manufacturing, robotics, smart automation, intelligent sensing, computer-integrated manufacturing, quality control, and advanced manufacturing processes.

Course topics can help students develop knowledge of precision machines, mechanisms, process machinery, material properties, manufacturing systems, and digital transformation in manufacturing.

Aerospace Engineering

Study the analysis, design, and development of aircraft, spacecraft, satellites, and rockets. Topics span aerodynamics, aerospace materials and structures, propulsion, flight dynamics, orbital mechanics, systems integration, and aerospace manufacturing.

The Aerospace Engineering area is organized around Aerodynamics, Dynamics and Control, Structures and Materials, and Vehicle Design, which can help guide course selection around specific interests.

Biomechanics

Apply mechanical engineering principles to biological systems and the human body. Topics include the musculoskeletal and cardiovascular systems, human motion, fluid and solid mechanics of tissues and organs, constitutive models, dynamics and motor control, and design considerations for medical implants and prosthetics.

This focus area connects mechanical engineering fundamentals with bioengineering and healthcare applications.

Fluid and Thermal Mechanics

Develop advanced knowledge in fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, and heat transfer. Topics include fluid flow, multiphase flows, heat exchange, computational fluid dynamics, combustion, mass transfer, renewable power systems, turbulence, boundary layers, and microfluidics.

Students can also explore thermal-fluid system components and applications such as turbines, pumps, valves, reactors, heat exchangers, piping systems, propulsion, HVAC, and energy systems.

Hypersonic Technologies

Study the multidisciplinary challenges associated with sustained hypersonic flight, including high-speed aerodynamics, propulsion, high-temperature materials, controls, and thermal management.

Topics include scramjet and ramjet engines, plasma flow interactions, boundary-layer transition, heat transfer, guidance and navigation, and technologies associated with hypersonic aircraft, re-entry vehicles, rockets, and space launch systems.

Mechanics of Materials and Structures

Develop an in-depth understanding of solid mechanics and its application to structural systems across scales. Topics include stress and strain, elasticity, plasticity, failure and fracture mechanics, fatigue, creep, advanced material models, vibration, and computational analysis.

Students can also explore multiscale modeling, material characterization, mechanical component design, structural testing, and finite element analysis across applications such as aerospace, automotive, marine, biomechanical, and other engineering systems.

Ocean Engineering

Study the design, construction, and operation of equipment and systems for marine environments. Topics include naval architecture, ocean structures, underwater vehicles, ocean observing systems, coastal engineering, and marine renewable energy.

Coursework can also address ship hydrodynamics, offshore platforms, subsea pipelines, ocean instrumentation, wave and tidal energy, and environmental monitoring.

Robotics, Dynamics, and Controls

Study robot motion planning, rigid and compliant body kinematics, trajectory optimization, robust and adaptive control, machine vision, mapping, and dynamics and control of robotic manipulators and mobile systems.

The focus area includes classical feedback control as well as modern optimal, adaptive, intelligent, and learning control methodologies, with applications across manufacturing, surgery, transportation, defense, and other domains.
